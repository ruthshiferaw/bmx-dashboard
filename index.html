COM motion processing

Measured COM motion is smoothed using a 15-frame Savitzky–Golay filter, mapped to track position using video landmarks, and fitted with a cubic smoothing spline (8 mm RMS target). The same spatial spline supplies the COM position and derivatives used in the dynamics.

Physics used in this dashboard

Physics used in this dashboard

On the ramp and its transition, the rider is approximated as a point mass (q=0 in the dynamics) to avoid applying the video-measured roller COM height to an unmeasured transition COM path. On rollers, q(x) is prescribed from synchronized video COM and landmark measurements. q̇=qₓvₓ; q̈=qₓₓvₓ²+qₓaₓ. The acceleration is solved self-consistently with the rolling force. The tangential equation is D·aₛ = mᵣΛ(κ′qvₛ² + 2κq̇vₛ) − g sinθ(Mg − mᵣκq) + Fpedal − Fdrag − Froll, with Λ=1−κq and D=Mbike+Iwheel/rwheel²+mᵣΛ². Drag = ½ρCdA vₛ|vₛ|; rolling resistance = Crr·max(N,0). Red points use piecewise-constant measured interval-average video speeds while the constrained-contact model predicts loss of contact.

Model limitations

Model limitations

The prescribed q(x) profile comes from one video, not an independently optimized pumping law. Spatial shifts and amplitude scaling are sensitivity experiments. When the constrained-contact model predicts negative normal load, horizontal speed is taken from the measured interval-speed profile (red regions). Physics integration resumes on re-contact. This is a validation bridge, not a flight-dynamics prediction. The COM signal is assumed to be a normal displacement, as in the coworker's original formulation; verify that against your digitization convention. Playback uses elapsed trajectory time, not frame index.
